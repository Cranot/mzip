=============================================================================
2. THE ROUTING MAP — every win and loss traced to a named gate
=============================================================================
Telemetry via MZIP_STATS=1. Container families compete whole-file
(MU/BG/MS/MT/MB/MQ/ML/MY/MF/MM/MI); block types compete per block.

input size          | container | encoder    | outcome
--------------------|-----------|------------|---------------------------
<= 64 KB            | MU (lite) | wraps      | LOSES to brotli by 0.10%
  (MU_MAX_SIZE)     |           | BROTLI     | -- it IS brotli + framing
64 KB - 1 MB        | standard  | PPMD       | WINS
  (MC_THRESHOLD=1MB)|           |            |
1 MB - 16 MB        | BG/MT/MY  | BWT_TEXT   | WINS
> 16 MB             | multi-blk | BWT_TEXT   | WINS, and margin GROWS
  (DEFAULT_BLOCK_SIZE=16MB)

Which family won, by corpus: BG (prose, dialogue, alpaca, tweets, gsm8k),
MT = GRID (tabular-numeric), MY = FILTER (code). This corroborates
ablation.md's load-bearing families (A5_GRID 418,875 B, A8_FILTER 645,475 B,
A4_NUMERIC 384,417 B) on independent data.

THE TWO LOSSES, AND THEY ARE DIFFERENT ANIMALS
- Tiny (<=64KB): mzip SELECTS brotli and pays container overhead on top. It
  cannot win by construction. Not worth fixing; gate by size and route small
  payloads to brotli directly.
- qa-arc (619 KB): -3.50% vs PPMd. This is the length-prefixed binary framing
  used for the per-column test, not the dataset. RE-TEST on arrow buffers
  before publishing any per-column claim.

3. SCALE BEHAVIOUR — the fear was wrong, mzip IMPROVES with size
=============================================================================
wikitext-103 slices, all three mutually comparable (same corpus).
PREDICTION ON RECORD: mzip's deficit would GROW past the 16MB block boundary
because it must split while a CM streams. That prediction is FALSIFIED.

slice     |  zstd -19 |  7z PPMd  |  lpaq1    |  mzip     | mzip bpb
----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|----------
16,776,925| 4,469,626 | 3,755,068 | 3,401,669 | 3,693,778 | 1.7614
25,165,395| 6,650,084 | 5,588,922 | 5,034,963 | 5,426,460 | 1.7251
33,553,232| 8,825,021 | 7,439,250 | 6,660,576 | 7,126,277 | 1.6991

mzip vs 7z PPMd : +1.63% -> +2.91% -> +4.21%   (mzip pulls AWAY)
mzip vs zstd-19 : +17.36% -> +18.40% -> +19.25% (widening)
lpaq1 vs mzip   :  7.91% ->  7.22% ->  6.54%   (CM gap CLOSING)
mzip bpb        : monotonically improving through the block boundary.

=> Multi-block splitting costs nothing measurable. mzip is a SCALE-UP story:
   the bigger the shard, the better it looks against everything.

=============================================================================
5. THE COST — this is the real productization blocker, not ratio
=============================================================================
compress throughput, 32 MiB slice:
   7z PPMd  0.18 s/MB    lpaq1 1.5 s/MB    mzip 17.4 s/MB (557.9 s total)
mzip is 93x slower than PPMd and 11.6x slower than lpaq to land BETWEEN them.
Decompression: ~11.7 MB/s vs zstd's ~1 GB/s (~100x), disqualifying for a
dataloader hot path but fine for cold archival.

AND MOST OF IT IS WASTE: on wikitext the winning stream is produced by bwt9 in
20.9 s (or cm_backend in 3.0 s) while the CLI takes 165.8 s -- ~87% of runtime
recomputes bwt9 across the per-block, split-trial and BG paths and runs
brotli/xz/PPMd backstops that all lose. An early-out on large text-like input
is 8-55x, free, no format change, no licensing question. THIS IS THE #1 FIX.
