MZIP vs WHAT HUGGINGFACE ACTUALLY RUNS
=======================================
measured 2026-09-01, i5-12500, single-threaded, /root/mzip-hfbench/
subject: /root/mzip HEAD e5fe7e8 + the NUM_EXTRACT guard (this session).
data: 8 MiB slices taken 4 MB into the tensor region of six real model files,
      fetched by HTTP range request. Every arm round-trip verified (ALL OK).
raw: weights/xet_full.json

WHAT HF ACTUALLY USES -- ESTABLISHED FROM SOURCE, NOT ASSUMED
---------------------------------------------------------------
xet-core/xet_core_structures/src/xorb_object/compression_scheme.rs:
    pub enum CompressionScheme { None = 0, LZ4 = 1, ByteGrouping4LZ4 = 2, Auto = 99 }
plus a sibling module `byte_grouping` implementing bg4_split / bg4_regroup.
So the Hub stores chunks as 4-byte-grouped + LZ4. Comparisons against RAW or
against zstd-19 are therefore NOT valid savings claims -- HF does neither.
lz4_flex's FrameEncoder defaults to FAST mode, so `lz4` at default level is the
faithful simulation; `-9` is included as the strongest the scheme could reach.
NOTE: Xet ALSO does chunk-level DEDUPLICATION across repos. Nothing here
measures that. These numbers are per-file compression only and would apply to
whatever survives dedup, not to gross uploads.

THREE FINDINGS WORTH STATING
------------------------------
1. PLAIN LZ4 DOES ALMOST NOTHING ON WEIGHTS: 1.010x. Xet's entire win on this
   data (1.191x) comes from the BG4 byte grouping, not from LZ4. Their design
   choice is the right one and it is doing ~95% of the work.
2. MZIP'S DECOMPRESS COST IS BIMODAL and the total conceals it. On gpt2 and
   bert-base mzip decompresses in 0.03 s -- the SAME as Xet. On SmolLM2, Qwen
   and bge-small it is 1.1-1.3 s (40-55x). It depends which encoder wins the
   trial. "24x slower" is the aggregate, not a universal property.
3. HF CAN HAVE ~8% FOR FREE WITHOUT US. bg4-lz4 at -9 reaches 1.288x against
   their current 1.191x, still at 12.5 MB/s compress and unchanged decompress
   (287 MB/s). That is a one-line config change on their side and it is worth
   saying out loud rather than hiding.

EXTRAPOLATION TO "4 PB UPLOADED LAST WEEK" -- WITH ITS ASSUMPTIONS NAMED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF the 4 PB were all model weights resembling these six, and IF the figure is
logical (pre-compression) volume, and IGNORING dedup:
    stored today at 1.191x : 4 PB / 1.191 = 3.359 PB
    stored with mzip 1.415x: 4 PB / 1.415 = 2.827 PB
    difference             : ~0.53 PB per week  (~530 TB), ~27.7 PB per year
    = the 15.84% figure, restated in petabytes.

EVERY ONE OF THOSE "IF"s IS LOAD-BEARING:
 - The 4 PB is "models AND datasets". The dataset half is parquet, already
   SNAPPY-compressed internally, and behaves completely differently. No
   measured models:datasets split exists, so a single blended percentage across
   all 4 PB would be fabricated.
 - Xet deduplicates across repos BEFORE storage. Compression savings apply to
   post-dedup bytes. The real figure is therefore LOWER than 530 TB, by however
   much dedup already removes -- which is unmeasured here.
 - Six 8 MiB slices is a small sample, and the per-model spread is 8.8-31.0%.
 - Slices are from the middle of each file, not whole files.

THE COST, WHICH MUST BE STATED ALONGSIDE THE SAVING
-----------------------------------------------------
mzip compresses at 0.164 MiB/s/core on this data. Sustaining 4 PB/week of
ingest would need roughly 38,000 cores running continuously -- versus Xet's
current ~237 MB/s, which needs a rounding error of one machine.
That single fact settles the framing: this is a COLD-TIER / ARCHIVAL argument
(write once, read rarely, keep forever), not a proposal to replace the upload
path. Decompression at 13 MB/s aggregate is likewise unfit for a hot serving
path, though the bimodality above means some model families decompress at
Xet-comparable speed.
Storage saved ACCUMULATES while compute is paid once per upload, so the trade
improves over a long retention horizon -- but that economics case is not
measured here and should not be asserted.
